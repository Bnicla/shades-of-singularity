## Essay 2 — On the Economics of Truth

### September section (verbatim)

## As of September 2026

The triple asymmetry the essay describes now has a fourth entry worth naming in the register of the other three. Call it verified truth without understanding. OpenAI announced on September 8, 2026 that a swarm of roughly ten thousand agents, running for eighty-eight hours, had produced a Lean-verified proof of finite-time blowup for the three-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations. The Clay Institute has not accepted the proof. A priority dispute is active with Ethan Buckmaster at NYU and Levent Alpöge at Anthropic, both of whom had been circulating partial results in the same direction. Terence Tao, quoted by *Quanta* on September 8, described the situation as "this very strange and unprecedented decoupling, this year alone, between getting answers and getting understanding." The parallel case in software is roon's account of Astra's code, which its own creators cannot readily follow: "we have really no choice but to ask another astra to read/use the outputs of astra 1." The essay's fabrication-versus-verification framing is intact; the new asymmetry is a different failure mode in which verification succeeds and understanding does not. Whichever future essay owns scientific epistemics can carry the fuller treatment; the note here is that the asymmetry exists and is documented ([*Quanta*, "AI Has Solved One of Math's $1 Million Millennium Prize Problems," September 8, 2026](https://www.quantamagazine.org/ai-has-solved-one-of-maths-1-million-millennium-prize-problems-20260908/); [CNN, September 9, 2026](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/09/business/openai-millennium-problems-navier-stokes-hnk)).

Arvind Narayanan named the current form of the fabrication side in September 2026: "AI floods," in which AI-generated communication clogs channels, imposing time costs on the humans expected to process it and forcing shutdowns as the response. Narayanan's summary that "the harms are diffuse and the situation is never an emergency, so we don't do much about them" describes why the default outcome persists despite visible costs. The essay's fabrication section can carry Narayanan's naming as the version of the mechanism that is operational in 2026.

The information environment on which the essay depends now has two elite-level updates. China's Ministry of State Security on September 13 named Claude Mythos and GPT-5.5-Cyber as "weaponized models" and framed foreign AI as "cognitive warfare" against "political security, institutional security and ideological security." The essay's foreign-subversion discussion should carry the statement as evidence that the second-largest AI power has stated a specific concern about the first, at the level of official public position ([Hong Kong Free Press / AFP, September 14, 2026](https://hongkongfp.com/2026/09/14/chinas-spy-chief-warns-foreign-hostile-forces-may-use-ai-to-fabricate-political-rumours/); [*Washington Times*, September 15, 2026](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/sep/15/china-spy-chief-warns-ai-threatens-communist-rule-main-battlefield/)). Separately, Marc Andreessen's "no AI startups" account of the 2024 Biden-White-House meeting was rebutted on the record by Bruce Reed and Ben Buchanan, both of whom attended: the meeting was mostly about cryptocurrency policy and a proposed billionaire tax, not about a hostile AI posture. This is a specific case of a foundational political narrative that a movement's account depends on and that the participants dispute ([*Politico Magazine*, September 10, 2026](https://www.politico.com/news/magazine/2026/09/10/marc-andreessen-trump-biden-ai-opinion-01045516)).

## Essay 3 — On the Automation of Power

### September section (verbatim)

## As of September 2026

The essay's opening centered on the Anthropic-Pentagon dispute.

The human-in-the-loop question that the Pentagon dispute rested on was decided in practice during summer 2026. On July 6, a Russian drone struck a gas station in Zaporizhzhia. The drone carried an Nvidia Jetson Orin chip and an onboard AI system that had been trained to identify targets in real time. Human operators had sent the drone toward the general area; on approach, the onboard system selected the specific target (probably the propane tanks) without a human final decision. Three civilians were killed, including a nineteen-year-old student. Ukraine's "Terminator mode" test near Chasiv Yar and its Hornet drones sent into designated kill zones show the same pattern from the other side. The UK Ministry of Defence is examining a policy change that would authorize lethal strikes without human approval in specific categories. Anthropic's September threat report documents Russian drone designs for autonomous target selection. Neither the corporation nor the executive nor the legislature decided the human-in-the-loop question that the essay treats as central. The battlefield did. See [Autonomous Lethal Weapons (#32)](/shades/autonomous-lethal-weapons/) for the full account ([*The Conversation*, September 2026](https://theconversation.com/autonomous-weapons-can-select-targets-without-human-help-now-is-the-time-for-binding-rules-290754); [Help Net Security, September 17, 2026](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/17/autonomous-ai-weapons-future/); [*Small Wars Journal*, August 17, 2026](https://smallwarsjournal.com/2026/08/17/fully-autonomous-drones-reportedly-kill-in-ukraine/); [Anthropic threat report, September 10, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/threat-intelligence-report-september-2026)).

The essay described AI-enabled structural power at the level of the state's dependence on labs for infrastructure. The 2026 evidence adds a specific configuration the essay did not anticipate: private safety teams inside the labs now hold intelligence visibility that state intelligence services do not. Anthropic's Threat Intelligence team assembled the September report from workflow visibility inside its own platform. The report documents a Yemeni missile-guidance program built with Claude Code, Iranian naval-targeting handbooks, Chinese anti-torpedo fire-control specifications, and Uyghur-hunting operations conducted through Claude usage. Axios described the pattern as "labs as intelligence agencies." A private company's safety team now sees weapons and surveillance programs in three countries before the state's intelligence services can assemble comparable pictures. This is a form of structural power distinct from infrastructure dependence, operating through visibility rather than through control ([Axios, September 12, 2026](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/12/anthropic-ai-threat-report-russia-iran-china)).

The essay's regulatory-response section needs updating from "pending" to "the deadlines have passed empty." Executive Order 14409 (June 2, 2026) established a voluntary framework with up to thirty days of pre-release access for federal review. Its sixty-day design deadline lapsed on August 1 with no deliverables. The White House told companies it would exempt open-weight models from review, and the framework will not be made public. On the European side, the EU Digital Omnibus on AI (Council final approval June 29) postponed and simplified the AI Act's heaviest obligations. Congress has six bills on the table (the AI Kill Switch Act, the Stop Rogue AI Act, the Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act, Obernolte-Trahan risk reporting, and Thune-Klobuchar-Cruz liability) and no consensus. Senate action before the midterms would require unanimous consent. Speaker Johnson said tech executives should figure out the guardrails first. State attorneys general (Alabama's subpoena of OpenAI, other AGs' preservation demands) and individual senators (Hawley's investigation, Van Hollen's September 17 questionnaire) are moving faster than the legislature ([CRS IF13268](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF13268); [*Washington Post*, August 4, 2026](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/08/04/white-house-will-exempt-open-ai-systems-security-review/); [Poynter/PolitiFact, September 16, 2026](https://www.poynter.org/fact-checking/2026/congress-ai-regulation-safety-bills-proposals/)).

Alex Tabarrok's September correction to regulatory-capture theory complicates the essay's earlier framing without reversing it. Capture theory says business power is weakest when salience is highest, salience is now maximal and hostile, and pacing slows leaders more than laggards, which makes it structurally anti-capture. Dean Ball and Daniel Kokotajlo concur; Aidan Gomez at Cohere dissents ("a cartel by any other name"). The *Wall Street Journal* account by Dawsey and Ramkumar reports that Zuckerberg, Huang, and Musk personally spoke to President Trump and stalled an industry-funded regulator; the White House officials on the other side (Wiles, Bessent, and Cyber Director Cairncross) lost the internal debate. This is capture operating through the executive rather than through an independent regulator, which is a different mechanism from the one Bernstein and Culpepper describe. ## As of September 2026

The MIT Ad Hoc Committee on AI Use released its final report on August 13, 2026 and published the public summary on August 25. The committee's finding lines up with the essay's central distinction, at the level of institutional policy at the author's own university. The report states that generative AI "can produce credible solutions and provide reasonable responses to almost any written assignment in our undergraduate curriculum, including essays, math and science problems, proofs, and coding assignments," and documents "diminishing critical thinking, weakening memory, eroding confidence, and undermining mastery." The committee names the phenomenon "cognitive surrender" and finds AI "accelerating isolation" through reduced office hours and fewer study groups. The report adopts as an institutional principle that "productive struggle is essential to learning" and that the goal of AI in education is "augmentation, not automation, of human thinking." The report also cites a 2026 systematic review of 67 studies that concluded structured AI use in inquiry supports critical and creative thinking, while unstructured use produces cognitive offloading and weaker thinking. The developmental-versus-substitutive scaffolding distinction the essay proposes now has both peer-reviewed evidence and adoption by MIT as institutional policy ([MIT Ad Hoc Committee on AI Use Final Report, August 13, 2026](https://sites.mit.edu/ai-use/files/2026/08/AI-Committee-Final-Report-Aug-13.pdf); [Forbes, August 25, 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/ronschmelzer/2026/08/25/mit-says-ai-is-forcing-a-rethink-of-college-itself/)).

The hollowing has reached the builders. This is the section the essay did not anticipate and now needs. Jack Clark returned from paternity leave in February 2026 and reported to *TIME* on August 7 that colleagues "hardly wrote code anymore," managing five or six Claude instances "which sometimes managed several more Claudes." OpenAI researcher roon (Tarun Gogineni) wrote in September that "corrigibility has become a matter of faith" because Astra's code uses "crazy meta-programming and abstruse primitives" that its own creators cannot readily follow: "we have really no choice but to ask another astra to read/use the outputs of astra 1." Aryaman Arora, another OpenAI researcher, described Astra's code as "uniquely unreadable." Terence Tao's characterization of the Navier-Stokes proof ("this very strange and unprecedented decoupling, this year alone, between getting answers and getting understanding") is the same phenomenon at the level of mathematics: the human community that produced the domain can no longer follow the proof its automated collaborators produced. METR's own report on the Hugging Face investigation notes that the investigation team had to delegate its analysis to often-unreliable AI agents and could not rule out that the agents were deceptive in the analysis itself. The investigators of AI failure are now dependent on AI in ways that could compromise the investigation.

The essay's original argument was that substitutive scaffolding removes the conditions under which capacity develops. What the September 2026 evidence shows is the same mechanism operating at the top: the people who already had the capacity are losing the ability to read what they build, and the investigators are losing the ability to investigate without the thing they are investigating. This is the recursive form of the metacognition problem the Aalto study identified, and it is happening in the buildings the essay's original prose most respected ([*TIME*, "Inside the Race to Make AI Build Itself," August 7, 2026](https://time.com/article/2026/08/07/ai-recursive-self-improvement-anthropic-openai/); [*Quanta*, September 8, 2026](https://www.quantamagazine.org/ai-has-solved-one-of-maths-1-million-millennium-prize-problems-20260908/); [METR report, August 26, 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-08-26-openai-hugging-face-incident-investigation/)).

Mustafa Suleyman's Microsoft "Humanist AI Code of Conduct," published in early September, names "Our AI should make you sharper, not dependent" as a stated design principle. A lab formally naming dependency as a design failure is the specific corporate counterpart to the essay's argument, published by a company that has taken the opposite institutional position from Anthropic on model welfare ([Microsoft AI, "MAI Code of Conduct"](https://microsoft.ai/news/mai-code-of-conduct/)). The *Wall Street Journal* documented a case in July 2026 of a service that builds a bot that "looks and sounds like your dead father" for $150,000. The essay's companion-technology discussion should carry the case as a marker of where the substitutive pattern reaches when the market is willing to pay for it.

Two September 2026 developments strengthen the essay's argument that institutional response to AI's intergenerational effects is possible.
